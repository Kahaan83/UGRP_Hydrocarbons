## 1. Problem Statement & Motivation

In fuel property prediction and inverse formulation, training individual property models in isolation frequently results in **physically contradictory predictions**:
- **Thermodynamic Impossibility**: An optimizer may predict a high flash point ($> 60\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$) while simultaneously predicting a low initial boiling point ($IBP < 100\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$), even though flash point is physically governed by the volatile front-end vapor pressure.
- **Physical State Contradictions**: An optimizer predicts a liquid kinematic viscosity of $4.5\text{ cSt}$ at $-20\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ for a blend whose freezing point is $-10\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ (at which temperature the fuel is a solid waxy precipitate that cannot flow).
- **Combustion Kinetic Violations**: Blends predicted with $> 40\text{ vol}\%$ aromatics simultaneously claiming a high Derived Cetane Number ($DCN > 60$), violating fundamental radical chain-branching combustion kinetics.

Task A6 resolves this by:
1. Empirically auditing the multi-property correlation structure across **2,695 real fuels** from the CRC and AJFTD databases.
2. Deriving and formalizing strict thermodynamic coupling rules.
3. Implementing an automated, production-grade `PhysicalConsistencyChecker` in `src/fuelblend/eval/consistency.py` to gate all candidate inverse designs and model predictions.

## 3. Physical Coupling Rules Codified

The checker enforces 4 core physical coupling invariants:

### 3.1 Rule 1: Flash Point vs Front-End Distillation (Mackison / API Correlation)
- **Thermodynamic Bound**: Flash point is the temperature at which the cumulative vapor concentration reaches LFL. Because $T_{10}$ is the temperature where 10% of the liquid has evaporated, the vapor concentration at $T_{10}$ is orders of magnitude above LFL. Therefore:
  $$T_\text{flash} < T_{10}$$
  A predicted $T_\text{flash} \ge T_{10}$ is an absolute physical violation.
- **API Technical Data Book Correlation**:
  $$T_\text{flash}^\text{expected} (^\circ\text{C}) \approx 0.683 \cdot T_{10} (^\circ\text{C}) - 66.8$$
  Predictions deviating by $> 35\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ from this regression are flagged as anomalous.
- **IBP Bound**: $T_\text{flash} < T_0 (\text{IBP}) + 15\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$.

### 3.2 Rule 2: Phase State & Rheological Consistency (Freezing vs Cold Viscosity)
- **Phase Boundary Rule**: If a fuel has a measured or predicted freezing point $T_\text{freeze} > -20.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$, it has undergone solid wax nucleation and crystallisation at $-20.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Violation**: Any claim that a blend has a liquid kinematic viscosity of $\le 8.0\text{ cSt}$ at $-20.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ while its freezing point is $>-20.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ is a physical state contradiction. Fluid flow ceases when the wax network forms.

### 3.3 Rule 3: Hydrocarbon Density-Viscosity Envelope (Walther-ASTM)
- Hydrocarbon jet fuel density spans $730\text{--}860\text{ kg/m}^3$ at $15\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$.
- Paraffins exhibit low density ($740\text{--}780\text{ kg/m}^3$) and higher viscosity. Aromatics exhibit high density ($860\text{--}920\text{ kg/m}^3$) and lower viscosity.
- Unphysical combinations (e.g. ultra-low density $< 730\text{ kg/m}^3$ with high cold viscosity $> 10\text{ cSt}$, or high density $> 860\text{ kg/m}^3$ with viscosity $< 1.5\text{ cSt}$) are rejected.

### 3.4 Rule 4: Combustion Kinetics (Cetane vs Aromatic Content)
- Aromatics possess high C-H bond dissociation energies and produce stable resonance-stabilized aromatic radicals, severely delaying radical autoignition (suppressing cetane number).
- High aromatic content ($> 30\text{ vol}\%$) cannot yield $DCN > 50$. Any blend claiming high aromatics and high cetane number is kinetically unphysical.

## 4. Software Architecture: `PhysicalConsistencyChecker`

Located at `src/fuelblend/eval/consistency.py`:
```python
from fuelblend.eval.consistency import PhysicalConsistencyChecker

checker = PhysicalConsistencyChecker()
result = checker.check_candidate_blend({
    "flash_point_C": 48.0,
    "distillation_T10_C": 175.0,
    "freezing_point_C": -47.0,
    "viscosity_minus20C_cSt": 4.5,
    "density_15C_kg_m3": 804.0,
    "aromatics_vol_pct": 18.0,
    "dcn": 45.0,
})

assert result.is_consistent is True
```

If an inverse optimizer generates a blend violating any coupling rule, `result.is_consistent` evaluates to `False` with specific explanatory strings in `result.violations`.
