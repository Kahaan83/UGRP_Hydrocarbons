## 1. Executive Summary

Task A4 re-evaluated the Le Chatelier + Raoult's law + Antoine flash point baseline across the complete corpus of **1,006 mixture records** from the Leenhouts et al. benchmark dataset.

Key findings:
1. **Coverage**: 126 of the 1,006 mixtures are pure hydrocarbons (`is_hc == True`), representing 31 distinct chemical systems. All 126 hydrocarbon mixtures achieved 100% convergence. The remaining 880 mixtures contain non-hydrocarbons (oxygenates, alcohols, esters) whose components are not in the hydrocarbon database (276 missing 1 component, 494 missing 2, 108 missing 3, 2 missing 4).
2. **Temperature-dependent LFL**: Zabetakis temperature correction $LFL(T) = LFL(25\text{ }^\circ\text{C}) \times [1 - 0.000721 (T - 25)]$ slightly reduces overall RMSE from 10.241 $^\circ\text{C}$ to 10.141 $^\circ\text{C}$ and cuts mean bias from +1.634 $^\circ\text{C}$ to +1.250 $^\circ\text{C}$, with minimal impact on MAE (6.156 vs 6.306 $^\circ\text{C}$).
3. **The C12+ Extrapolation Bug**: The overall MAE of ~6.2 $^\circ\text{C}$ obscures a dramatic bifurcation:
   - For light/medium hydrocarbons ($< \text{C12}$ only, $n = 48$), the physics baseline performs brilliantly: **MAE = 1.372 $^\circ\text{C}$** with temperature-dependent LFL (1.560 $^\circ\text{C}$ with constant LFL)!
   - For mixtures containing $\ge \text{C12}$ hydrocarbons ($n = 78$), the baseline degrades sharply to **MAE = 8.984 $^\circ\text{C}$** (Const) / **9.343 $^\circ\text{C}$** (Temp).
   - High-temperature Antoine parameterizations (e.g. for isododecane / 2,2,4,6,6-pentamethylheptane) severely underestimate vapor pressure when extrapolated to ambient temperatures, leading to a catastrophic +42.43 $^\circ\text{C}$ over-prediction of the flash point.
4. **Safety-Critical Directionality**: In fuel safety, predicting a higher flash point than actual ($pred > measured$) is the **dangerous direction** because a fuel will ignite at a lower temperature than expected. In the evaluated hydrocarbon mixtures, 27.0% suffer from dangerous over-prediction, with 19.8% (25 blends) exceeding actual flash point by $> 5.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ (worst case: $+42.43\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$).

## 2. Methodology & Mathematical Formulation

### 2.1 The Baseline Equation
Flash point $T_\text{flash}$ (in K) is the temperature satisfying the Le Chatelier vapor flammability condition:
$$H(T) = \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{y_i}{LFL_i(T)} = \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{x_i P_i^\text{sat}(T)}{P_\text{total} \cdot LFL_i(T)} = 1.0$$
where:
- $P_\text{total} = 101.325\text{ kPa}$
- $x_i$ is liquid mole fraction ($\sum x_i = 1$)
- $P_i^\text{sat}(T) = 10^{A_i - \frac{B_i}{T + C_i}}\text{ kPa}$ (Antoine equation)
- $LFL_i$ is lower flammability limit (as volume / mole fraction in vapor)

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent LFL
When enabled, Zabetakis' empirical rule adjusts LFL as a function of temperature:
$$LFL_i(T) = LFL_i(298.15\text{ K}) \cdot \max\left(1.0 - 0.000721 \cdot (T - 298.15), 0.05\right)$$
For $T > 25\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$, $LFL(T) < LFL(25)$, lowering the temperature required to achieve $H(T) = 1.0$ (lowering predicted flash point). For sub-ambient flash points ($T < 25\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$), $LFL(T) > LFL(25)$, raising predicted flash point.

The dataset divides into two distinct regimes:

2. **Heavy Hydrocarbons ($\ge \text{C12}$ present, $n = 78$ mixtures)**:
   - Constant LFL MAE: **8.984 $^\circ\text{C}$**
   - Temperature-Dependent LFL MAE: **9.343 $^\circ\text{C}$**
   - Drives almost all gross errors in the dataset.

### 3.2 Worst-Case Outlier Investigation: Row 132
- **Mixture**: 10 mol% Mesitylene (1,3,5-trimethylbenzene, C9 aromatic) + 90 mol% 2,2,4,6,6-Pentamethylheptane (Isododecane, C12 iso-paraffin).
- **Actual Measured Flash Point**: $45.85\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Baseline Predicted Flash Point**: $88.28\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ (Error: $+42.43\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$).
- **Root Cause**:
  1. Mesitylene pure flash point is $52.8\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$.
  2. Isododecane pure flash point is experimentally $\sim 45\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ (boiling point $177\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$).
  3. However, the WebBook Antoine constants for isododecane ($A=6.136, B=1782.3, C=-92.5$) were regressed from elevated temperature data ($> 100\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$). When extrapolated down to $45\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$, the Antoine equation predicts a pure flash point of **$100.4\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$**!
  4. Le Chatelier's rule with Raoult's law thus computes a mixture flash point of $88.3\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$, falsely assuming isododecane is practically non-volatile at $50\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$.

## 5. Architectural Implications & Recommendations for Modeling

1. **Safety Objective Function**: Because over-prediction of flash point leads to catastrophic safety hazards, the machine learning residual model must be trained with an **asymmetric loss function** (e.g. pinball loss or asymmetric Huber loss) penalizing $pred > actual$ substantially more than $pred < actual$.
2. **Vapor Pressure Extrapolation Guard**: The flash point baseline should not extrapolate Antoine equations $> 20\text{ K}$ below $T_\text{lo}$. Instead, Clausius-Clapeyron integration with measured or group-contribution $\Delta H_\text{vap}$ should be used for sub-ambient extrapolation of heavy components.
3. **Non-Ideality (Activity Coefficients)**: For alkane-aromatic blends, positive deviation from Raoult's law ($\gamma_i > 1.0$) causes mixtures to flash lower than ideal. Regular solution theory (Scatchard-Hildebrand) or UNIFAC should be introduced into the baseline.
