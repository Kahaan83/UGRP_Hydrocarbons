## 1. Executive Summary

Task A5 resolves the reliance on synthetic fuel distillation claims by extracting, standardizing, and assembling all available real-fuel distillation measurements across 4 major open experimental databases:
1. **CRC Report 647**: 57 aviation fuels characterized with ASTM D86 (both Test 1 and Test 2) and ASTM D2887 (Simulated Distillation / GC-based true boiling point).
2. **CRC Report AV-27-18**: 19 aviation jet fuels characterized with ASTM D2887 (5% cut increments from T0 to T100).
3. **Fortin et al. 2012 (Energy & Fuels)**: 14 rocket propellant fuels (11 orthogonal RP-1 blends, 1 standardized RP-1 4572, and 2 RP-2 fuels) measured with the NIST Advanced Distillation Curve (ADC) method.
4. **AJFTD (FAA/ASCENT)**: 99 alternative jet fuel candidates measured with ASTM D86.

Total standardized dataset: **303 distillation curves** across **189 real fuels**.

### 1.1 The Thermodynamic Gap Between "Ideal Boiling Point Mixing" and Real ASTM D86
Previous literature and early v1 models assumed distillation could be predicted via "ideal boiling point mixing" (linear averaging or cumulative chromatographic True Boiling Point [TBP] distribution like ASTM D2887).

Our rigorous paired evaluation on the 57 CRC 647 fuels (where both ASTM D86 and ASTM D2887 were measured on the exact same fuel samples) reveals that **ideal boiling point mixing fails catastrophically to predict ASTM D86 curves**:
- **At Initial Boiling Point (IBP / T0)**: ASTM D86 is **$+50.54\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ HIGHER** than the ideal boiling point distribution ($RMSE = 51.12\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$).
- **At T10**: ASTM D86 is **$+17.24\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ HIGHER** ($RMSE = 17.35\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$).
- **At T50**: ASTM D86 matches D2887 reasonably well (Mean bias: $+2.61\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$, $MAE = 4.20\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$).
- **At T90**: ASTM D86 is **$-15.06\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ LOWER** ($RMSE = 15.17\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$).
- **At Final Boiling Point (FBP / T100)**: ASTM D86 is **$-42.78\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ LOWER** than the true end point ($RMSE = 43.35\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$).

By measuring the experimental repeatability between ASTM D86 Test 1 and Test 2 on the exact same fuels ($MAE = 0.49\text{--}1.65\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$), we prove conclusively that this $+50\text{ }^\circ\text{C} / -43\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ error is **pure thermodynamic/methodological bias**, not experimental noise.

### 3.2 Physical Root Cause of the Discrepancy
1. **Initial Boiling Point (IBP)**: In an ASTM D86 Engler distillation flask, approximately 1 theoretical stage exists with zero fractionation. The first liquid drop dropping into the receiver occurs only after the entire vapor neck has heated to equilibrium and vapor condensate fills the condenser tube. In ASTM D2887 gas chromatography, any trace light component elutes immediately, registering a much lower initial boiling temperature.
2. **Final Boiling Point (FBP)**: ASTM D86 test ends when the bottom of the flask runs dry or decomposes into smoke, leaving 1.0--1.5 vol% residue. The heaviest high-boiling compounds never reach the vapor thermometer. In D2887, high-temperature capillary GC columns elute compounds up to C44+ without residue, yielding a significantly higher true end point.

### 3.3 Evaluation of RP-1 Surrogate Model (NISTIR 6646) vs Measured RP-1 (Fortin 2012)
The NIST 14-component surrogate mixture for RP-1 was evaluated against the measured Advanced Distillation Curve (ADC) of RP-1 batch 4572:

The 14-component surrogate contains light C9 components added to match bulk density and molecular weight, which causes the ideal boiling distribution to under-predict the front end of the distillation curve by up to 66 $^\circ\text{C}$.

## 4. Modeling Implications

1. **Never conflate ASTM D86 and ASTM D2887**: Models trained on simulated distillation cannot predict ASTM D86 without an explicit nonlinear stage conversion (e.g., the Riazi-Daubert or API Technical Data Book Procedure 3A1.1 conversion).
2. **Ground Truth Validation**: All future distillation models must benchmark against the 303 standardized curves in `distillation_real_fuels.csv`, holding out whole fuel groups via GroupKFold on `source` or `fuel_class`.
