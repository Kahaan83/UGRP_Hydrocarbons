## 1. What This Project Is Trying to Do

When designing or evaluating aviation fuels (like Jet A, JP-8) or rocket propellants (like RP-1), engineers must ensure the fuel meets strict physical and safety standards:
1. **Kinematic Viscosity:** How easily the fuel flows and pumps through aircraft engines, especially in extreme freezing conditions at high altitudes.
2. **Flash Point:** The lowest temperature at which vapors above the fuel can ignite. This is a critical safety threshold (typically > 38 °C for jet fuel).
3. **Density:** How heavy the fuel is per unit volume, which dictates aircraft payload, range, and combustion stoichiometry.
4. **Distillation Curve & Cold Flow:** How the fuel evaporates across boiling temperatures (ASTM D86) and when it starts freezing or solidifying.

### The Big Problem in Earlier Work
Earlier machine learning approaches often trained neural networks on **synthetic blends** where the labels were generated by formulas (like the Refutas viscosity equation). When a model is trained on synthetic formulas, reporting "0.4 % error" is circular—the neural network is simply memorizing the equation, rather than learning the actual physics of real molecules. When tested on real fuel samples, these models often fail.

### Our Solution
1. Collect and clean **real experimental laboratory measurements** from major world databases (NIST TRC, ThermoML, Leenhouts benchmark, CRC aviation fuel reports, and the FAA/Illinois AJFTD database).
2. Establish rigorous **physics baselines** (Refutas blending for viscosity, ideal volume for density, and Le Chatelier + Antoine vapor pressure for flash point).
3. Train **residual machine learning models**: let known physics do 90 % of the work, and train machine learning to predict only the non-ideal molecular interactions (the "residual").
4. Strictly hold out entire molecular families to test true extrapolation to novel molecules, ensuring zero data leakage.

## 2. Where We Are Right Now (Summary of What Was Done)

We have built, verified, and locked the entire end-to-end data and modeling pipeline:

### A. The Unified Experimental Dataset
- **Over 101,000 raw experimental measurements** ingested across 8 open scientific repositories.
- **76,175 clean, de-duplicated measurements** in our canonical database (`data/unified/measurements_dedup.csv`), covering 1,038 unique pure hydrocarbon compounds and thousands of binary/multicomponent blends.
- **Full AJFTD Scraper Ingestion:** We successfully processed **3,102 saved HTML pages** from the Illinois/FAA Alternative Jet Fuels Test Database (AJFTD), extracting **91,175 rows of data across 2,679 unique aviation fuels**.
- **Rule R1 Applied:** We enforced a strict data-quality barrier (Rule R1). Blends without defined molecular structures (such as uncharacterized raw petroleum cuts) are automatically routed into an isolated benchmark set (`data/processed/excluded_by_R1.csv`, 33,513 rows). The clean molecular model table (`data/processed/model_table.csv`) contains **42,662 high-quality hydrocarbon records**.

### B. Pure-Compound Machine Learning Models (WP4)
For novel molecules where laboratory tests have never been performed, we trained a 5-seed ensemble of Gradient Boosted Decision Trees on 22 quantum/structural molecular descriptors:
- **Viscosity:** Natural-log RMSE of 0.25 on held-out molecules.
- **Density:** MAE of 10.2 kg/m³ (~1.2 % error).
- **Flash Point:** MAE of 0.56 °C.
- **Vapor Pressure:** Log10 RMSE of 0.23 across wide temperature ranges.

### C. Mixture Physics Baselines & Residual Models (WP3 & WP5)
We evaluated the classical physical equations against our held-out test set (S2, which contains molecules never seen during training):
- **Viscosity:**
  - Standard Grunberg-Nissan log-linear baseline: Log-RMSE = 0.1225.
  - Our Mixture Residual Model (learning excess intermolecular friction): Log-RMSE = **0.0781** (a **36.2 % reduction in error**).
- **Density:**
  - Ideal volume baseline: RMSE = 2.48 kg/m³ (~0.3 % error).
  - Our Mixture Residual Model: RMSE = **2.46 kg/m³** (nearly identical to the baseline, confirming that hydrocarbon liquid mixing is virtually ideal with respect to volume).

### D. Flash Point Safety & Conformal Prediction (WP6)
In aviation safety, **over-predicting flash point is dangerous** (predicting 42 °C when the fuel actually ignites at 36 °C can lead to explosions).
- We implemented a **conformal safety layer** that calibrates the lower bound of flash point predictions.
- On our independent test split, the calibrated safety margin achieved **92.0 % empirical coverage**, effectively eliminating dangerous optimistic errors.

### E. Real-Fuel Surrogate Bridge (WP7)
For real commercial fuels (Jet A, JP-8) that only have refinery inspection test sheets rather than pure chemical recipes:
- Standard refinery correlation (Nelson $T_{10}$ equation): Mean error = 8.26 °C.
- Our Multi-Metric Bulk Model (using full distillation curve + bulk density + aromatics): Mean error = **3.36 °C** (a **59.3 % reduction in error**).
